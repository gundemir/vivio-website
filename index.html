Therapeutic & genome-engineering design

Engineered antibodies, allogeneic cell products, clonal iPSC strategies and therapeutic function.

+

We develop therapeutic concepts and the plans needed to test them. Our work covers engineered antibodies, including bispecific formats, and cell therapies. Target selection, immune compatibility, manufacturability and the route to a reproducible product are considered from the outset.

Allogeneic cell therapies

Immune compatibility & clonal products

Hypoimmune engineering & missing-self recognition

Reducing HLA expression can limit T-cell recognition while exposing a cell to NK-cell “missing-self” responses. We assess how a hypoimmune strategy addresses both, and what evidence is needed to evaluate persistence, retained function and safety.

Single-clone-derived cell therapeutics

We plan the development of products derived from a defined engineered iPSC clone: starting-line suitability, clone selection and qualification, cell banking and differentiation. Genomic integrity and potency need to be assessed in the resulting therapeutic cells, alongside consistency across batches.

Product and manufacturing feasibility

Cell source, engineering complexity and differentiation choices affect the number of functional cells a process can deliver. We include viable yield, recovery after thawing, release requirements and cost per usable dose in candidate comparisons.

Therapeutic genomic design

Target recognition, function & control

CAR and target-selection strategy

Our design experience includes NK cells, T cells, regulatory T cells and dendritic cells. We assess target distribution and the intended immune effect, including how recognition could distinguish diseased tissue from healthy or repair-supporting cells.

Gene editing & regulated expression

We compare knockout, targeted integration and gene-regulation approaches against the function required. Design work includes multi-gene constructs, safe-harbour integration and conditional expression, with attention to whether the added complexity is justified.

Safety controls & candidate validation

We define how inducible safety controls, potency and selectivity should be assessed. Where suitable, transient expression can test a design before stable clone development. Matched comparators help establish what each modification contributes.

Typical outputs

A target and modality assessment, an engineering rationale, a clone-qualification plan and a preclinical study plan with candidate-selection criteria.

Preclinical research

Disease models & tissue selectivity

We design studies to distinguish effects on diseased tissue from effects on healthy or repair-supporting cells. Model choice follows the candidate’s mechanism and intended indication.

  • Human tissue, organoids & organ-on-chip
  • Mechanistic controls & potency assays
  • Efficacy, biodistribution & safety planning

Indication strategy

Cancer, immune disease & ageing

Our research concepts address tumour recognition, antigen-specific tolerance and pathological senescence. In chronic disease, we consider how the tissue environment affects cell function and the capacity for repair.

  • Antigen-specific immune regulation
  • Senescent-cell clearance & tissue repair

The Vivio valuation model

From scientific evidence
to financial decisions.

A promising result matters when it changes a development or commercial assumption. Our methodology makes that connection explicit for therapeutic assets and the startups developing them.

01 / The comparison

Start with a relevant reference.

Therapeutic class, indication and stage define the historical comparison. An antibody and a cell therapy can face very different development and manufacturing questions.

02 / The evidence

Explain what changes.

Assess what the programme’s evidence adds, where it overlaps and whether it changes a probability, timeline, cost or commercial assumption.

03 / The decision

Show what drives value.

Compare the conventional and Vivio cases, test scenarios and identify the assumptions most capable of changing the conclusion.

For founders, developers and investors
An assessment of the science, a documented valuation, a comparison of the two approaches, and a clear view of the evidence and funding still needed.
