Public methodology

The science behind
the valuation.

A therapeutic valuation should explain how scientific evidence changes the financial assumptions. The Vivio model brings those decisions into one documented assessment.

Overview updated 2 November 2026 · Builds on the public white paper, v0.3

1. Choose the right comparison.

We consider therapeutic class, indication and development stage together. A historical average is useful only if its programmes are relevant to the asset being assessed. Cell source, antibody format, patient selection and target maturity may require a closer comparison.

A sample of the questions in the class-by-indication matrix
Therapeutic classOncology & haematologyImmune & inflammatory disease
Engineered antibodiesTarget selectivity, resistance and comparative benefitDurable control, chronic exposure and infection risk
Cell therapiesPersistence, tumour access and deliverable supplyImmune regulation, persistence and long-term safety
Small moleculesExposure, selectivity and competing treatmentsExposure, tolerability and chronic use

These are assessment prompts, not probabilities or automatic scoring rules. The public paper contains the fuller classification.

2. Identify what the evidence changes.

Target validation, exposure, safety, manufacturing and trial design can affect different assumptions. We examine whether a finding is relevant to this product and population, whether it is already represented in the historical reference, and whether another observation has already captured the same information.

A regulatory designation, novel mechanism or positive study is not a fixed valuation multiplier. The assessment must explain the consequence for the programme.

Room for expert judgement.

Some features of a therapeutic are too specific to be captured well by standard inputs. A bounded expert-judgement adjustment allows us to account for these features, drawing on Vivio’s experience in therapeutic design, development and finance.

The adjustment starts at zero and can be positive or negative. We document the feature, the reasoning and the assumption it changes, and check that its effect has not already been counted elsewhere. Its contribution should be meaningful without dominating the valuation. Any probability adjustment remains subject to the model’s overall limits.

We show the valuation with and without this adjustment. Its limits and usefulness will be challenged through backtesting as the historical evidence base grows.

3. Follow the spending and the calendar.

Committed spending is different from a later study that proceeds only after a successful result. We link costs to the decisions that incur them, and map the development calendar to the actual work. Better early evidence can raise both expected proceeds and expected later spending.

4. Check what can reach patients.

The eligible population, uptake, reimbursement, usable manufacturing output and treatment capacity jointly constrain sales. Chronic treatments, finite courses and durable one-time therapies require different patient-flow assumptions.

5. Explain the result.

We show the conventional case alongside the Vivio assessment and identify the sources of the difference. Sensitivity analysis tests individual assumptions; scenarios combine changes that could occur together. Product value is distinguished from company equity value and the return to a particular investor.

A living model, refined with new data and challenged by backtesting.

The Vivio model is designed to evolve as evidence accumulates. New data are reviewed in batches, with changes introduced through documented model updates. Each new input does not automatically change the mathematics.

We are assembling the historical evidence needed for backtesting: comparing what the model would have predicted, using information available at the time, with what subsequently happened. This will help us test proposed refinements and identify where judgement needs correcting.

Sources, evidence dates, assumptions and model versions are retained so an assessment can be reviewed and reproduced. Expert judgements remain explicit; we do not present them as empirically validated coefficients.
